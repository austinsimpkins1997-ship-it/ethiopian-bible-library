## Boundary observations

A line-pattern scan of the plain-text file, from its first chapter opening to the printer’s colophon, found 102 line-leading Roman-numeral headings followed by verse marker `1`. The query did not find that exact pattern for 3, 4, 35, 38, 39, 44, or 59, and found it twice for 32. These are pattern results, not a count of missing chapters.

The HTML rendition confirms that chapter III is present, but begins “III. Observe…” without a numbered `1`. The HTML also has explicit grouped headings/ranges: I–XXXVI; XVII–XXXVII; XXXVII–LXXI; LXXII–LXXXII; LXXXIII–XC; XCII–CV; CVI–CVII; and CVIII. Its grouped headings place XCII and XCI together, then present their material in more than one subsection. These editorial groupings help explain why a simple `Roman numeral + 1` search is inadequate.

The HTML and plain-text evidence do not yet establish a complete, ordered chapter map. The source’s grouped divisions and chapter-specific headings must be parsed and reconciled with the printed edition before a chapter reader or alignment is built. This audit does not assert that the print edition or the underlying work omits any numbered chapter.
