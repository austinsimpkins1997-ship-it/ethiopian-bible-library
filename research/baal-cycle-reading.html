COMPARATIVE LITERATURE · TRANSLATION REGISTER

The Ugaritic Baal Cycle

This page records the books recommended for studying Baʿal and the ancient Ugaritic texts. It is a research guide, not a translation of those books or a claim that the damaged tablets survive in full.

Translation boundary: the listed modern books are already written in English. Their full text cannot be republished here as a new translation. The archive can provide concise original summaries and, as source material and specialist review allow, a separately labeled English rendering of the ancient Ugaritic text. Missing or restored words must remain visible; uncertain readings must not be presented as certain.

Books and what each contributes

Ugaritic Narrative Poetry, edited by Simon B. Parker

An English-language scholarly edition of major Ugaritic story-poems, including the Baal Cycle, Kirta, and Aqhat, with Ugaritic text, translations, and notes. A practical first source for reading the ancient stories in context.

Publisher record (SBL Press)

Stories from Ancient Canaan, translated and edited by Michael D. Coogan

A shorter English collection of ancient Canaanite/Ugaritic stories, including Baal material. It is a reader’s introduction, not a full critical commentary.

Library catalog record

The Ugaritic Baal Cycle, Volume I, Mark S. Smith

A specialist edition with text, translation, introduction, and commentary for KTU 1.1–1.2.

Brill publisher record

The Ugaritic Baal Cycle, Volume II, Mark S. Smith and Wayne T. Pitard

A specialist edition with text, translation, and commentary for KTU 1.3–1.4. It is not the full cycle by itself; the surviving poem continues through KTU 1.5–1.6, and those tablets also require a critical edition.

Brill chapter record

The Early History of God: Yahweh and the Other Deities in Ancient Israel, Mark S. Smith

A scholarly historical study of Israelite religion and its relationship to other ancient West Semitic traditions, including Baal. It is an argument based on evidence, not a direct translation of the Baal Cycle.

Eerdmans publisher record

Mighty Baal: Essays in Honor of Mark S. Smith, edited collection

Specialist essays on Baal, Ugaritic religion, and later reception. Individual chapters address different questions; the collection is not a continuous translation of the tablets.

Brill chapter and contents record

Ancient-text basis

  • The Baal Cycle is conventionally identified as KTU 1.1–1.6 and survives in damaged tablet witnesses from Ugarit (Ras Shamra).
  • The University of Göttingen’s EUPT project is building a digital philological edition of Ugaritic poetry: tablet facsimiles, transliteration, vocalization, translation, grammatical analysis, and commentary. Its corpus page currently schedules the Baʿlu Cycle editions for 2027–2029 and says editions are released gradually. At the 2026-10-25 check, this is a prospective project source, not a complete online Baal Cycle edition. The timetable may change.
  • Translations must distinguish legible text, editorial restoration, and lost text. A smooth English sentence can conceal uncertainty, so the archive should retain brackets and notes.
  • The Baal Cycle is comparative ancient literature. It is not a Dead Sea Scroll and is not listed here as part of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo canon.
  • The Ginsberg translation shown in a user-provided scan was identified as part of Pritchard’s 1969 anthology. That modern translation is not reproduced here; reuse rights remain unresolved.
