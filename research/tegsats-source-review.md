# Tegsats (Reproof): source and boundary review

## What the sources support

- The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church's undated English page lists Proverbs as Old Testament entry 24 and “Tegsats (Reproof)” as entry 25. That directly supports preserving them as separate list entries; it does not give either entry's chapter boundaries. [Church list](https://www.ethiopianorthodox.org/english/canonical/books.html?lang=en)
- G.A. Mikre-Sellassie's 1993 article reports, in printed p. 120 note 51, five “books of Solomon” in an Amharic commentary on Fetha Negest: Messale (Proverbs 1–24), Tegsas (Proverbs 25–31), Wisdom, Ecclesiastes, and Song of Songs. The article discusses multiple historical canon lists; this passage is an attributed report, not an official contemporary ruling or a verified mapping to a particular edition. [Article PDF](https://translation.bible/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/mikre-sellassie-1993-the-bible-and-its-canon-in-the-ethiopian-orthodox-church.pdf)
- The web-hosted *Encyclopaedia Aethiopica* entry “Wisdom Books, biblical” gives a different traditional division: Méssale 1–24 and Tägíasä Sälomon 25–29. This conflicts with the 1993 article's note and must remain visible as a source disagreement. [Encyclopaedia Aethiopica entry](https://en.sewasew.com/p/wisdom-books-biblical)
- Oxford's thesis abstract says Hugh Pilkington's 1978 critical edition uses Cambridge Add. 1570 as its base, collates 21 Ethiopic manuscripts and two printed sources, and includes a literal English translation of the base text. It reports LXX-dependent chapter/verse structure and mixed Greek/Hebraic features; these are the thesis author's scholarly conclusions, not independently re-evaluated here. Oxford lists two OCR PDFs and copyright dated 1978. A separate reference index links “Ethiopic Book of Rebukes” to pp. 318–522, but the thesis files and those pages have not been inspected. The index is a discovery pointer, not proof that those pages represent the Church-list Tegsats entry. [Oxford thesis record](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3Ae7c60bb3-3fc4-4b10-941f-8065617acbfa) · [Secondary reference index](https://israelite-biblical-canons.neocities.org/pages_for_biblical_texts/proverbs)
- The British Library catalogs Add MS 16186 as a Geʽez/Ethiopic parchment codex dated 1600–1699 and lists “Tagśāṣa Salomon” at folios 22r–26rb, corresponding in its catalog to Proverbs 25–29. The catalog page footer separately licenses its text under CC BY except where otherwise stated; this is not assumed to license manuscript images or extracted scripture. A 335,223-byte IIIF Presentation 3 manifest is stored locally with a checksum-pinned capture record. Its 159 canvas metadata includes f.22r–f.26v, but no image bytes or scripture text were fetched. Its top-level `rights` value is present but blank; the embedded usage statement does not resolve image access/reuse. [British Library record](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/032-003433717) · [digitized-content viewer](https://iiif.bl.uk/uv/#?manifest=https://bl.digirati.io/iiif/ark:/81055/vdc_100132593851.0x000001) · [local IIIF manifest](readers/source/tegsats-bl-add-ms-16186/iiif-manifest.json) · [capture record](readers/source/tegsats-bl-add-ms-16186/capture-manifest.json)

## Archive treatment

The archive preserves the Church's exact entry title and list position. It does not assert that 25–29 or 25–31 is the definitive range for the complete Church-listed work. Add MS 16186's 25–29 range is attributed to its manuscript catalog record only. Oxford's abstract supports a literal English rendering within Pilkington's critical edition of Ethiopic Proverbs, but neither it nor the secondary index establishes that this English text is a translation of the separately listed Church-book Tegsats. The translation and thesis remain unbundled and copyright terms are unresolved. The catalogue-record text is CC BY as stated in the footer; rights and access for manuscript images and extracted scripture remain unresolved. The local IIIF file is metadata only. No witness text or images were copied, no chapter count or completeness is claimed, and the entry stays without a reader; the 81-entry list and canon-linked reader count do not change.
