# Book of Clement — Qälementos source review

The Church’s English list names “The book of Clement” at New Testament entry 34 but does not provide a Geʽez title. [Alessandro Bausi’s 2024 chapter](https://iris.uniroma1.it/retrieve/01e7c267-8bf0-4164-ab76-d8df7fdaa2f0/Bausi_The-Ethiopic-Book-of-Clement_2024.pdf) identifies the Ethiopic *Maṣḥafa Qalemǝnṭos* (“Book of Clement,” CAe 1957), distinguishes it from the Ethiopic Apocalypse of Peter (CAe 317), and describes a stable seven-book structure that only partly corresponds to the Arabic Apocalypse of Peter to Clement. The shared English title makes Qälementos a plausible candidate, but does not establish that it is the exact work intended by the Church-list entry.

Bausi reports complementary French and Italian translations covering the work: French books I–II, part of III, and excerpts from other books; Italian books III–VII. He says a comprehensive critical edition remains outstanding. These are bibliographic reports, not text files audited here. The chapter also cautions that a recent Geʽez–Amharic edition is not an official EOTC edition and may adapt or rewrite its source. No manuscript image, scripture transcription, or translation is bundled.

The chapter itself states CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 terms: noncommercial redistribution of the unaltered publication with attribution. That license does not grant rights in the manuscript or the Qälementos texts discussed in the article. Their rights remain unassessed. The archive’s Bausi source record is `SRC-207`; the canon card keeps the crosswalk uncertain and the reader status separate from text coverage.

## Beta maṣāḥǝft work record (metadata and short incipits, not a reader)

The commit-pinned [LIT1957Mashaf TEI record](https://github.com/BetaMasaheft/Works/blob/330a89d34e4088d2eaabb5b87101dbf054457166/1001-2000/LIT1957Mashaf.xml) identifies CAe 1957 with Geʽez, normalized, and English title forms for *Maṣḥafa Qalemǝnṭos / Book of Clement*. The file includes an introduction, seven numbered Book divisions, and short Geʽez incipit divisions; it has no chapter/verse hierarchy and is not a continuous full-text reader. A metadata-only capture record is retained [here](../readers/source/clement-betamasaheft/capture-manifest.json); the TEI text itself is not copied.

The file states CC BY-SA 4.0. Current Beta maṣāḥǝft Guidelines state CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 for project data/remixes, and the University of Hamburg's dataset-level record uses CC BY 4.0 with file-specific terms. Their relationship for this TEI's reuse is unresolved. The lead therefore remains metadata-only, rights-mixed, and separate from verified Church-list coverage. See sources SRC-424, SRC-296, and SRC-297.

## English print-edition lead (not yet text-reviewed)

The Ethiopian Orthodox Bible Project states that an English translation of “Ethiopic Clement” is available for purchase and explicitly warns readers not to confuse it with 1 Clement. Its statement identifies neither the translator nor the source witness in that page. [Project bibliography/claim](https://ethiopianorthodoxbible.wordpress.com/2024/08/25/beware-of-complete-and-81-book-ethiopian-bibles-in-english/)

The linked retailer listing identifies *The Book of Clement from the Ethiopian Bible: The First English Translation of Peter’s Visions Given to Clement*, author Breandan Lumpkin, publisher Fifth Estate, Incorporated, publication date 5 December 2024, 236 pages, ISBN 978-1958450154. These are retailer-supplied metadata; the book’s contents, source witness, translation path, boundaries, completeness, accuracy, and reuse terms were not inspected. [Retail bibliographic listing](https://www.amazon.com/Book-Clement-Ethiopian-Bible-Translation/dp/1958450154)

This is an acquisition and review lead only. The title and secondary description do not prove that the 2024 volume translates the same Ethiopic work Bausi identifies as *Maṣḥafa Qalemǝnṭos* (CAe 1957), nor that it matches the Church list’s exact “Book of Clement” entry. A separate Fifth Estate article describes a forthcoming revised reader edition and says complete Books V–VII lie outside its scope; that is a publisher statement about the announced revised edition, not verified evidence about the 2024 print volume. The article says chapter 12 follows a short summary of Books V and VII, then refers to the full translation of V–VII being outside the edition; this wording is retained as an unresolved source inconsistency. [Publisher scope note](https://www.fifthestatepub.com/post/ethiopian-bible-81-books-explained) No text is copied or bundled; rights remain unknown. The catalogue mapping stays uncertain and this lead does not increase bundled-reader coverage.

## Separately announced revised reader edition

Fifth Estate’s article says a revised reader edition is “coming soon” and outlines twelve modern chapters. It maps chapters 1–5 to manuscript Book I, chapters 6–7 to Book II, chapters 8–10 to Book III, and chapter 11 to Book IV. The publisher says chapter 12 follows a short summary of Books V and VII, while its next sentence says the complete translation of Books V–VII lies outside the edition. The scope note records that inconsistency as published and does not resolve it by inference. The article does not confirm publication, ISBN, source manuscript, accuracy, or reuse rights. These are editorial chapter divisions, not verified manuscript boundaries. [Fifth Estate article](https://www.fifthestatepub.com/post/ethiopian-bible-81-books-explained)
