Gamo and Maale: what the source records establish

This note distinguishes the identified editions from unknown translation history, separate product records, and changing license notices. It reports the publisher and catalogue statements inspected for this archive; it is not a translation review or legal opinion.

Translation basis remains unstated

The inspected eBible.org edition records identify Gamo/Gamotso (gmv) and Maale/Male (mdy), their displayed scripts, credits, and reuse notices. Neither record names the language from which its scripture text was translated or any relay-language path. The archive therefore records those fields as not stated. It does not assume that either translation was made from Geʽez, Amharic, English, or another language. This is missing provenance in the inspected notices, not evidence of a particular translation method. See SRC-168 and SRC-363.

Gamo / Gamotso (GMVETW / gmve)

The eBible.org edition page identifies Gamo/Gamotso (gmv), Ethiopic script, copyright © 2024 The Word for the World International, and contributor The Word for the World Northern Africa. It states CC BY-SA 4.0 for the text and separate terms for pictures. The archive's retained mobile HTML package has 1,323 HTML pages; its visible menu has 66 labels. Those counts describe stored pages and observed menu labels, not a verified 66-book canon crosswalk, print completeness, translation accuracy, or source-language basis. Images, fonts, and CSS are excluded. See SRC-168, SRC-169, and SRC-178.

The Word for the World's 2022 project-history article reports that the Gamo full-Bible dedication took place in August 2022, that a Gamo New Testament was completed in January 2012, and that Gamo translators, checkers, pastors, elders, and church partners participated after a long translation project. It does not name individual translators, specify the source or relay language, identify a specific edition, or establish that the captured 2024 edition exactly matches the dedication edition. This adds attributed process history, not a text-level verification. See SRC-375.

A separate 2011 Gamo New Testament record

eBible.org separately catalogs Ooratha Caaqo Maxaafa Gamoththo as a Gamo New Testament under ID GMVWBT / gmvRNT, with a 2011 Wycliffe copyright notice. Its page contains two license-version statements: CC BY-NC-ND 3.0 in the initial copyright information and CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 in a later New Testament block dated 2014-04-25. The archive preserves this discrepancy, does not redistribute the text, and does not infer which version governs. The catalog page does not state the translation's source or relay language or name the script in its metadata; the displayed sample verses use Latin orthography, so Latn is recorded provisionally for that witness. This record is kept distinct from the 2024 GMVETW / gmve Ethiopic-script full-Bible edition; the 2012 completion milestone does not prove those texts are identical. See SRC-397.

Maale / Male (MDYTWF / mdyeth)

The live eBible.org page identifies the Ethiopic-script Maale Bible, language tag mdy, © 2008 The Word for the World International and Bible Society of Ethiopia, translator The Word for the World, and contributor Bible Society of Ethiopia. The page calls it “Maale Full Bible,” links the USFM download retained by this project, states CC BY-SA 4.0, and was last updated 13 May 2025. The retained archive has 66 USFM book modules and 30,506 observed verse labels; these are package and extraction counts. They do not prove printed-edition completeness, translation accuracy, or equivalence to the EOTC or Eritrean Orthodox lists. Images are omitted under the provider's separate media terms. See SRC-363 and SRC-364.

The translation agency's current team biography names Tamene Lale Gego and says he translated a complete Bible into his mother tongue Maale. It does not date that work or link him to the 2008 MDYTWF / mdyeth edition; the archive preserves the biography as language-level attribution evidence and leaves the edition's source-listed translator as The Word for the World. The biography also does not give a source language or relay path. See SRC-374.

Maale Revelation 3:20 translation example

A separate agency article dated 12 December 2022 reports that Maale translators rendered the door-and-knocking scene with a garden gate and a visitor calling out to someone in the house. The article says this reflects local visiting practice. This is useful evidence that translators may preserve a passage's communicative scene through culturally familiar wording rather than a mechanical word-for-word match. That is the agency's account, not an independent linguistic or community review by this archive. The article names neither the source/relay language nor the specific edition or translators, so it does not establish that this wording appears in the bundled 2008 MDYTWF / mdyeth text. The archive does not certify equivalence. See SRC-398.

License-version discrepancy: a third-party mirror carries an older copyright page with the same Maale title and credits but displays CC BY-ND 4.0. Its footer says the page was generated through eBible.org on 21 February 2024 from source files dated 14 April 2023. The currently inspected eBible.org MDYTWF / mdyeth page, last updated 13 May 2025, links the retained USFM package and states CC BY-SA 4.0. The archive records both dated notices, uses the current directly linked publisher notice for its stated package terms, and does not claim to resolve why the notice changed or any legal effect on earlier copies. See SRC-372.

A different product code is not a crosswalk

The Digital Bible Society has a separate record, MDYBSE, describing a 2008 Maale Bible, labeling its scope “full protestant canon,” and showing the license as “RESERVED.” It is not the eBible.org identifier MDYTWF / mdyeth. No textual identity, license transfer, or Orthodox canon equivalence is inferred between them; the record adds no reader coverage. See SRC-373.

What this changes in the archive

  • Translation source language and relay path remain explicitly unknown for both editions.
  • The Maale source still credits The Word for the World; an agency biography names Tamene Lale Gego as translating a complete Maale Bible, but does not identify this edition.
  • The Gamo agency history documents community translation process and milestones, but not the precise identity or source-language path of the captured 2024 edition.
  • The Maale package keeps its current publisher-linked CC BY-SA 4.0 metadata, while the dated CC BY-ND mirror notice is now discoverable as a rights-history discrepancy.
  • The separate MDYBSE product is not merged with MDYTWF / mdyeth.
  • The 2022 Maale Revelation 3:20 example is stored as language-level process evidence only; its edition identity and translation path remain unknown.
  • Gamo and Maale readers remain research records without EOTC/Eritrean Orthodox canon links. No scripture text or reader coverage was added by this audit.
