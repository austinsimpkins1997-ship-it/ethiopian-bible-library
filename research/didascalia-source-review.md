# Ethiopic Didascalia — source discovery review

The [EOTC English canon page](https://www.ethiopianorthodox.org/english/canonical/books.html?lang=en) includes the Church-list title Didascalia. In its explanatory section, the same Church page describes the Ethiopic Didascalia as containing the first seven books of the Apostolic Constitutions and says its subject matter differs from Latin, Arabic, and Syriac versions. This is the Church page's own description; it does not identify the Platt or Harden witnesses as the exact current Church-list edition. Asale's discussion is attributed comparative scholarship, not a Church ruling. [TAU's Didascalia notes](https://www.tau.ac.il/~hacohen/Didas/Information.html) identify the 1834 Platt edition and describe TAU's copy as an image version; the [TAU menu](https://www.tau.ac.il/~hacohen/NTBiblia.html) exposes an introduction, sections I–XXII, and notes. Four menu targets were sampled, each as a single-image wrapper. The [MDZ catalogue](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/details/bsb10218768) identifies Platt as editor/translator, lists Geʽez and English, and marks the digitized object NoC-NC. The [Qatar National Library catalogue](https://ediscovery.qnl.qa/islandora/object/QNL:00012804) independently records the 1834 title, Platt attribution, English translation in the title, and print extent (2 unnumbered pages, xvi, 131 pages, 21 unnumbered pages); its Rights field says ‘No Known Copyright’. The [QNL disclaimer](https://ediscovery.qnl.qa/disclaimer) warns that materials may remain subject to third-party rights and requires users to determine reuse conditions. This metadata does not clear global reuse. Its [official IIIF manifest](https://api.digitale-sammlungen.de/iiif/presentation/v2/bsb10218768/manifest) enumerates 178 scan canvases labelled (0001)–(0178), matching the catalogued scan count. A local copy of the manifest metadata and its SHA-256 are stored [here](../readers/source/didascalia-platt1834/capture-manifest.json). The independent [Internet Archive item](https://archive.org/details/ethiopicdidascal00platrich) also provides an OCR derivative; the raw TXT and XML are stored [here](../readers/source/didascalia-platt1834/internet-archive-djvu.txt) and [here](../readers/source/didascalia-platt1834/internet-archive-djvu.xml), with checksums and limitations in the [capture manifest](../readers/source/didascalia-platt1834/internet-archive-capture-manifest.json).



A separate edition, J. M. Harden's The Ethiopic Didascalia (1920), is catalogued by [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Ethiopic_Didascalia.html?id=944gAAAAMAAJ) as an English work of 204 pages and is held in a [Cornell scan at Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/cu31924096083336). Its raw Tesseract TXT and page-structured XML are stored locally [here](../readers/source/didascalia-harden1920/djvu.txt), [here](../readers/source/didascalia-harden1920/djvu.xml), and [here](../readers/source/didascalia-harden1920/capture-manifest.json). The provider checksums match; the scan front matter states that no known copyright restrictions apply in the United States. This is not an explicit open license or a global rights determination. Harden's Introduction (pp. xvii–xviii) reports 43 chapters in four of five manuscripts and says Platt's manuscript joins IV/V under one title. The local OCR contains 43 main-text chapter-heading occurrences. All 43 headings were visually checked against the corresponding scan pages; the [structured audit](didascalia-harden1920-chapter-heading-audit.json) records the confirmed I–XLIII sequence and corrects OCR numeral errors on PDF pages 87 (X, OCR xX) and 165 (XXXI, OCR XXXII). The [chapter-indexed OCR research view](didascalia-harden1920-ocr-reader.html) exposes grouped OCR lines for inspection; its body text has not been proofread. Scan completeness has not been reviewed, and the audit does not establish the Church-list work's chapter structure or identity. These are edition-specific observations, not a verified reader.

A separate scholarly context source, [Mebratu Kiros Gebru's 2012 doctoral thesis](https://www.collectionscanada.gc.ca/obj/thesescanada/vol2/OTU/TC-OTU-35210.pdf), describes the Ethiopic Didascalia as 43 chapters: chapters 1–32 correspond with variation to the 27 Syriac Didascalia chapters, while 33–43 include material from Apostolic Constitutions VI–VII. It discusses competing accounts of the Ethiopic text's ancestry and suggests an Arabic basis; that genealogy is an attributed scholarly interpretation. Gebru reports that Platt's English translation contains only the first 23 chapters, while Harden's is complete. This clarifies that Platt cannot serve as a whole-work English reader, but the claim has not been checked against the scans and the TAU menu's I–XXII labels remain unexplained. No text is added. Source SRC-423.

The [Arquivo Histórico da Igreja Lusitana authority record](https://arquivo.igreja-lusitana.org/e1izg) lists John Mason Harden's dates as 1871–1931 and marks its own description preliminary and minimal. This is a biographical lead for the 1920 translator, not a proven identity or a copyright determination; the archive's text and images are marked as restricted for reuse. No image or extended archival text is bundled. Source SRC-429.

The menu count is not proof of the Church-list work's scope, completeness, canonical classification, or textual accuracy. Platt is credited as translator, and the recorded Geʽez-to-English path is based on the title and catalogue metadata, not text comparison. TAU's notice is conditional on noncommercial reuse with the required notices; image-specific and jurisdiction-specific reuse still needs review. The scan manifest confirms only the digital object's listed canvases, not completeness of the printed volume or Church-list work. The Internet Archive OCR remains unreviewed: TXT has no Ethiopic Unicode or page breaks, XML has 186 page objects while the item page reports 190 pages. It is not a Geʽez transcription or reader. The US-only public-domain claim reflects the provider's 1834/no-visible-notice metadata, not an explicit open license or a global legal determination. No reader or canon-linked coverage is added.
