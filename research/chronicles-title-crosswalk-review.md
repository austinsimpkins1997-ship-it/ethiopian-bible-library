# Chronicles title crosswalk review

**Scope:** reviewed work/title matches for catalogue discovery only. These matches do not turn any research reader into a canon-linked reader, identify an EOTC or Eritrean edition, or establish textual equivalence.

## Evidence and decision

The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church's cited English list names **I Chronicles** and **II Chronicles** as separate Old Testament entries 11 and 12. The official USFM reference defines `1CH` as **1 Chronicles** and `2CH` as **2 Chronicles**; it also records **1 Paralipomenon** and **2 Paralipomenon** as Orthodox alternative titles. The retained source members or Gamo publisher pages identify the corresponding modules with those exact codes. The shared title/code identity is therefore reviewed, not merely inferred from a navigation-name lookup.

This evidence establishes only which titled work a source module claims to contain. It does not establish EOTC/Eritrean affiliation, a particular edition or recension, Geʽez or Church-edition identity, identical contents or chapter boundaries, translation ancestry, completeness, or accuracy. Every reader remains a separately scoped research witness and remains excluded from canon-linked coverage.

## Discovery records

The archive indexes nine bundled research-reader candidates for each entry (nine for I Chronicles and nine for II Chronicles). The records preserve each item's language, edition, rights classification, source location, and review limits. Eight candidates per entry are keyed to retained standard-code source members; the Gamo candidates are keyed to the publisher's retained `1CH`/`2CH` pages. These are candidate counts, not nine EOTC translations or nine verified editions.

The separate Beta maṣāḥǝft Geʽez Chronicles TEI snapshots are **not** part of those bundled-reader counts: the wording is withheld in this public build while file-level and project-level rights notices remain unresolved. Their title, declared Geʽez edition division, chapter structure, source hashes, and rights conflict are retained in the withheld-text metadata and source audits; no public reader text is supplied.
