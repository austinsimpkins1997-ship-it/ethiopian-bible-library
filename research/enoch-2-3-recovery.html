2 Enoch and 3 Enoch

These are separate ancient works associated with Enoch, not missing chapters of 1 Enoch. This page restores source access while distinguishing a historical OCR capture from a verified reader.

2 Enoch · Slavonic Enoch · Secrets of Enoch

A local source transcription is available

The archive stores the Internet Archive OCR of The Book of the Secrets of Enoch (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1896), translated from Church Slavonic by W. R. Morfill and edited by R. H. Charles. The file is the OCR of the whole scanned volume: it includes front matter and editorial material as well as the translated work.

This is not a verified chapter reader. OCR was captured byte-for-byte from the source item and has not been collated against the page images. OCR character errors, layout artifacts, and the edition's chapter/recension choices remain. The historical translation is not a direct translation from a newly checked Slavonic manuscript. Modern scholarship recognizes more than one recension and reports Coptic fragments; origins and textual history remain debated.

3 Enoch · Sefer Hekhalot · Hebrew Book of Enoch

The 1928 edition is located; its translation is not bundled

The Internet Archive provides a digitized copy of Hugo Odeberg's 3 Enoch; or, The Hebrew Book of Enoch (Cambridge University Press, 1928), with English translation and Hebrew text. Its catalog describes a translation with notes and a Hebrew text with critical notes, based on Odeberg's thesis; the edition identifies Bodleian MS Opp. 556 as its base manuscript.

“3 Enoch” is a scholarly title; it does not appear as the work's title in the manuscripts. The work belongs to the Hebrew Hekhalot literature. The translation is under copyright in some jurisdictions, and the digitizing library says its reuse rights were not evaluated. For those reasons, this project links to the scan but does not copy or present its English text as freely reusable or universally cleared.

Canon and scope

The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church's numbered list has an entry called “Enoch.” This archive's reviewed title match for that entry concerns 1 Enoch. The list does not give separate numbered entries for 2 Enoch or 3 Enoch; this project makes no canon claim for either work. Their inclusion here is comparative textual research, not a claim that they belong to the Ethiopian Orthodox canon.

“Recovered” here means the historical edition and its source trail are accessible. It does not mean lost originals have been found, every manuscript witness has been recovered, or all variants have been translated.
