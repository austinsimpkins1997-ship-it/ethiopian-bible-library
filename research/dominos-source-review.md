# Dominos source review

## Evidence

The [EOTC canon page](https://www.ethiopianorthodox.org/english/canonical/books.html?lang=en) lists the first and second Books of Dominos. The [Ethiopian Orthodox Bible Project's canon page](https://ethiopianorthodoxbible.wordpress.com/ethiopian-orthodox-canon-of-scripture/) glosses these as Books of the Covenant I and II. The [Lexham Bible Dictionary](https://biblia.com/books/lbd/word/Book_of_the_Covenant%2C_Ethiopian?ssi=0) describes the Ethiopian Book of the Covenant as a two-book work and identifies the fuller title Testament of Our Lord Jesus Christ. Its separate [Sinodos contents entry](https://biblia.com/books/lbd/article/SINODOS%245FWRITING.CONTENTS) warns that manuscript contents vary.

Beta maṣāḥǝft records the Geʽez [Testamentum Domini, CAe 2461](https://betamasaheft.awhamburg.de/works/LIT2461Testam/main) as a 60-chapter work in two parts, and separately catalogs [Testamentum Domini in Galilea, CAe 2462](https://betamasaheft.awhamburg.de/works/LIT2462Testam/main) as a composite work with a Galilee apocalypse and the Epistula Apostolorum. The Ethiopian Orthodox Bible Project's [book-edition note](https://ethiopianorthodoxbible.wordpress.com/category/buy-eotc-books/) says its First Dominos edition uses Cooper and Maclean and its Second Dominos edition uses M. R. James; it asserts textual proximity to Geʽez but this archive has not checked that assertion against the editions or manuscripts.

## Archive treatment

The two Beta works are entered as uncertain candidate crosswalks for the two Church-list entries, preserving the distinction between a Geʽez work record and an exact canon mapping. No book text is bundled, translated, or counted as reader coverage. Edition identity, recension, internal boundaries, completeness, and reuse rights remain unverified. The broader *Maṣḥafa kidān* record CAe 1938 is not silently treated as either Dominos book because its catalogued contents (Kidān za-nagh and Kidān za-sark) are not these two complete works.
