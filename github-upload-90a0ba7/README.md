# Ethiopian Bible Library — static site

Mobile-first and offline-capable. All catalogue, source, related-text, and reader data are embedded in `index.html`; the site makes no external data requests. On GitHub Pages or another HTTPS host, a service worker caches the app shell after a successful initial load and refreshes its saved copy during later online visits. Install it from the browser menu or share sheet where supported. A direct `file://` copy also works offline, but browsers do not allow service workers or install prompts for local files. Offline browser storage can be cleared or evicted by the device. Source links open only when a reader chooses them and need an internet connection. External citations display the listed URL host; a remote page may redirect to another host, and this archive does not host or security-scan third-party downloads. Do not bypass browser security warnings.

The footer downloads the catalogue, source records, translation registry, and bundled readers as `ethiopian-bible-library-archive.json`, directly from the copy embedded in the page. Export works offline and does not fetch a separate data file. The committed `archive.json` is the matching build input; the build check keeps it identical to the embedded app data. Bundled texts retain their individual reuse conditions, documented in `CONTENT-LICENSES.md` and the source records. The reader panel separately exports the selected text layer, or both displayed witnesses, as a self-contained JSON package with the selected text, its language/basis records, linked sources, review limits, rights notices, and any canon mapping explicitly labeled. This export works offline and does not grant reuse permission; its schema is `schemas/reader-package.schema.json`.

The build also writes `archive-manifest.json` with SHA-256 and byte length for every package file except the manifest itself, Git metadata, Python bytecode, and `__pycache__`. The manifest is linked from the site and precached with the offline shell. After downloading or mirroring the package, run `python verify_archive_manifest.py` from this directory to check for missing, added, or changed files.

The proposed multilingual and worldwide expansion is described in [GLOBAL-ARCHIVE-PLAN.md](GLOBAL-ARCHIVE-PLAN.md). This page is a static EOTC-focused prototype, not a global scripture database or a complete collection.

The reader separates five views: the bundled text witness or translation; the registered language and translation paths; the edition and its historical context; the documented canon and interpretive notes; and an integrated view that places these distinct records together. The integrated view does not blend unlike translations or claim a universal interpretation. Registered languages are not all world languages, catalogue titles are not automatically translated, and unavailable text remains unavailable until its source, boundaries, basis, rights, and review are recorded. At this release, eleven of the Church-list entries have canon-linked source-text readers. The eight locally bundled Geʽez Octateuch readers (Genesis–Ruth) have separately identified English Brenton Septuagint companions; Psalms has one as well. These historical English layers are based on Greek, not Geʽez, and matching chapter/verse labels are navigation aids only. The six companions for Psalms and Leviticus–Judges were parsed from checksummed eBible.org USFM members, retaining notes, cross-references, translator additions, paragraph markers, descriptive-title markers, and unnumbered continuation text. Job, Tobit, and Sirach also have separate English Brenton research readers from the same package. Job has 42 chapter divisions, 1,082 verse labels, and 2 unnumbered continuation segments; Tobit has 14 divisions, 245 labels, and 2 continuation segments. Each count is measured from its selected digital member. Sirach has 51 chapters and 1,368 observed labels; its index page 0 contains only a heading, while page 1 contains the prologue text and chapter 1 labels. Thirty-eight additional books and textual sections from the same checksummed USFM package are available as separate English research readers. Their headings and chapter divisions follow the archived source labels; no EOTC crosswalk is assigned. Parsing preserves Lamentations’ opening paragraph before verse 1, Kings footnote markers whose source note has no text, and Proverbs 30:1 as a source label with its note but no fabricated verse body. This includes distinct Greek Esther and Daniel texts, additions, and 1–4 Maccabees, whose matching titles do not establish same-edition or same-canon identity. All 42 research readers remain outside canon-linked coverage. These texts are not Geʽez transcriptions or EOTC edition identifications. Extraction checks do not verify print-edition completeness or translation accuracy; no English Brenton text is presented as a verified Geʽez translation.

Coverage numbers are computed from the actual reader records bundled in the built archive, not from a manually maintained display count or generated wording. The site reports both bundled and not-bundled counts from that inventory. “Not bundled here” describes this archive and does not mean no edition exists elsewhere. Every language profile carries an explicit external availability survey state. A `none_found` result is valid only for a completed, bounded, dated survey with cited sources and an observed count of zero; unassessed availability remains `not_surveyed`, never zero. The source's own labels and observed counts are retained, including gaps or irregularities. Unknown perspectives stay marked unknown or unsurveyed until attributed evidence is added. Machine-generated text remains a labeled draft, not a source witness, coverage evidence, or verified translation.

The portable record format is a draft v2 [text-layer JSON Schema](schemas/text-layer.schema.json), with explicit source-transcription, published-translation, generated-draft, human-composition, and unknown text origins. The build rejects generated drafts and unknown origins as bundled scripture. The [downloadable metadata manifest](examples/portable-text-layers.json) contains 24 selected records: 11 source readers, 9 historical English companions, Job/Sirach/Tobit English research translations, and one separate Mark TEI source transcription whose language remains unresolved (`und`). Counts and boundaries describe observed bundled content; no gaps are silently normalized, and missing or unknown viewpoints and wording are not filled by generated text. The builder checks language, script, origin, unit counts, and checksums against local reader data. English companions are bundled for Genesis–Ruth; they are distinct historical translations from the Greek Septuagint, not from the accompanying Geʽez texts, and shared labels do not establish alignment. Exodus and Ruth came from browser-visible page extracts; Leviticus–Judges are extracted from five checksummed USFM source members. ## Locally bundled reader

The web reader contains eight source-text books from the historical Ethiopic Octateuch edition edited by August Dillmann (1853–1855) and digitized/transcribed by Ran HaCohen: Genesis (50 chapters), Exodus (40), Leviticus (27), Numbers (36), Deuteronomy (34), Joshua (24), Judges (21), and Ruth (4). It also contains a 151-page Geʽez Psalter transcription from Ludolf's 1701 edition, digitized and retyped by Ran HaCohen at TAU. Psalter pages follow the online Rahlfs numbering; source marker irregularities are preserved. TAU warns transcription errors may remain, and this archive has not independently collated the text. The Genesis–Ruth source is not presented as the current official EOTC printed edition. The digitizer reports OCR followed by proofreading and warns that transcription errors may remain; the archive has not collated the text against manuscript witnesses. HaCohen’s Octateuch digitization converts Dillmann’s Ethiopic verse numerals to Arabic numerals and moves them inline; the displayed numbering follows the Septuagint and can be discontinuous where the Ethiopic text omits a Septuagint verse. The local reader preserves those displayed labels without claiming that the underlying transcription was independently collated. A separate Geʽez Jubilees transcription and the EOTC-labeled Geʽez 1 Enoch block are also bundled from pinned TEI records; their edition and rights boundaries are recorded separately. Brenton’s English Septuagint is bundled as a distinct historical English companion for Genesis and Exodus, not as a translation of either Geʽez witness. The Genesis companion is structurally checked against its 50 local page captures; the Exodus companion is an unreviewed research transcription parsed from browser-visible extracts, with raw HTML and original response bytes not retained. For the 146 Leviticus–Ruth chapter captures, a repeatable audit confirms each local HTML file matches the archived capture byte-for-byte and that parsing those pages reproduces the stored chapter text, source labels, and URLs. This verifies local capture-to-reader fidelity only, not the upstream transcription's accuracy or completeness.

Beside it is Brenton’s 1851 English translation of the Greek Septuagint: eBible.org identifies this translation as public domain. The archive records that as a provider claim, not a worldwide legal determination. It is a historical English companion, **not a translation from the bundled Geʽez transcription**. The offline reader preserves 1,532 English labels, including lettered labels, and 197 translator/editor notes. It pairs passages by printed labels for navigation only and keeps unmatched labels visible; matching labels do not establish identical wording. The two witnesses have separate source and reuse notes.

The digitizer’s notice permits copying and distribution of the Ethiopic transcription for noncommercial purposes when the copyright and permission notices accompany every copy. Commercial rights are not granted. eBible.org identifies Brenton’s 1851 English translation as public domain; the archive records the provider’s statement rather than making a worldwide legal determination.

The coverage index lists the 46 Old Testament and 35 New Testament entries on the EOTC English canon page. In this build, every one of those 81 entries links to at least one catalogue research row, and every catalogue row is referenced by the navigation crosswalk; build validation rejects unmapped entries or rows. This complete navigation index does not verify textual identity or canon crosswalks. Eleven of those entries have bundled Geʽez web-reader text: Genesis through Ruth, Psalms, Jubilees, and 1 Enoch. Genesis and Exodus have separately identified English companions based on the Greek Septuagint. The Psalter reader structurally audits 151 ordered TAU pages and 2,537 nonempty numbered units; duplicate, skipped, and nonnumeric labels are retained. That verifies page and extraction structure only, not textual accuracy, completeness against the 1701 print or manuscripts, or an exact Church-list crosswalk. The 1 Enoch reader uses only the pinned Beta maṣāḥǝft TEI block labeled EOTCed, identified there as the EOTC printed-Bible text. A local structural audit found chapters 1–108 and 1,058 labeled lines; chapter 21 starts with source marker 2, which is preserved. Source Text independently displays a 1,058-verse count, but no line-by-line collation was done. The exact physical print exemplar/date and transcription accuracy remain unverified. The separate restricted scholarly edition in the TEI is excluded, and the full upstream TEI snapshot is retained locally outside the Pages site while the public repository contains an EOTCed-only extract. A CrossWire SWORD Geʽez module is archived locally; its structure is decoded only far enough to audit its OSIS chapter identifiers and counts against the reader. No verse text is imported from the module or claimed as collated. A locally generated inventory records the eight Octateuch books. Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy, Joshua, Judges, and Ruth have source-linked chapter pages; verse markers were checked against links on local digitizer pages. This does not independently verify transcription accuracy, manuscript completeness, or an English translation. The 81-entry list is not a claim that all texts or translations are present; catalogue crosswalks remain separate and may need review.

The generated `index.html` embeds the catalogue, all 15 reader records (11 canon-linked and 4 research-only), source records, and translation registry in one local data block. English companion layers are stored with their reader records. The service worker precaches that page and the app shell and falls back to the cached page for offline navigation. Reading bundled content therefore needs no runtime data request; external evidence links still require a connection. `tests/test_offline_bundle.py` checks that the embedded data matches `archive.json`, that all bundled reader chapters contain text, and that the app does not fetch reader data at runtime. Run `node tests/test_reader_package.js` to check source-only, translation-only, parallel, and research package exports. These checks verify packaging and fallback wiring, not a browser-level offline session.

## Canon and completeness boundary

The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church’s English canon page states 81 canonized books (46 Old Testament, 35 New Testament). This archive has 90 searchable research rows because titles may be split/indexed for research; 90 is not a canon count, and many mappings remain unverified. Genesis through Ruth, Jubilees, and the EOTCed 1 Enoch block have bundled Geʽez text. The other catalogue records do not imply a complete text is available. This is not an official Church publication. Read the edition and permission sources in the page’s Sources & method section before reusing the text.

The site preserves this specific, undated Church English page as its 81-entry navigation list. Canon scholarship is shown separately: Bruk Ayele Asale’s 2016 article and 2015 dissertation, and G.A. Mikre-Sellassie’s 1993 comparison of broader and narrower canon lists, discuss uncertain contents, names, and counting. The dissertation enumerates four Sinodos titles that align at title level with the Church list’s four transliterated labels and notes that manuscript links between Proverbs and Tegsas are unclear. Mikre-Sellassie documents historical differences in grouping Proverbs and Maccabees and names Sirach in a separate list. These sources provide attributed context, not rulings on the Church page or proof of its edition-level crosswalks; uncertain identities remain explicit (sources SRC-029, SRC-072, SRC-073, SRC-101).

### New Testament source limits

TAU’s Platt 1830 information pages separately identify the Pauline Epistles (pp. 560–598) and Catholic Epistles (pp. 627–674), with page-listed epistle families. Both say the online transcription by Ted Erho is for personal research only and cannot be stored in another repository without written consent. No text was copied. The Pauline page has an inconsistent heading (“The Catholic Epistles”) despite a Pauline-Epistles body; the archive records that discrepancy. These pages establish a source-edition lead, not chapter boundaries, completeness, or an exact EOTC canon crosswalk, so the reader count remains unchanged. Sources: [Pauline Epistles information](https://www.tau.ac.il/~hacohen/NT/InformationPa.html), [Catholic Epistles information](https://www.tau.ac.il/~hacohen/NT/InformationEp.html).


### Books of Reigns candidate editions

TAU’s Dillmann-based edition page directly equates Reigns I–II with Samuel I–II and Reigns III–IV with Kings I–II. It dates the fascicles to 1861 and 1871, describes OCR followed by proofreading, and cautions that new typos were introduced. The archive now lists two separately scoped, provisional metadata leads for Church-list entries 9 and 10. Chapter boundaries, completeness, accuracy, current EOTC edition identity, and specific file-level rights have not been verified. No text was copied; the 11-of-81 reader count is unchanged. TAU’s site-wide terms are noncommercial and notice-bound. [Source information page](https://www.tau.ac.il/~hacohen/Regum/Information.html).

Hadiyya (`hdy`), Nuer (`nus`), and Wolaytta (`wal`) now have edition-level metadata leads: a 1992 Bible Society of Ethiopia Hadiyya Bible, a 1998 Bible Society in South Sudan Nuer Bible, and a 2004 SIM Wolaytta edition. The cited sources describe Hadiyya and Nuer as full Protestant-canon editions, not EOTC canon mappings; eBible describes Wolaytta as “Portions.” Hadiyya reuse terms are not established, Nuer is marked RESERVED, and Wolaytta is CC BY-NC-ND with no changes to words/punctuation; none is bundled. A separate GitHub repository filename list is preserved only as a discovery pointer, and its MIT software license is not treated as scripture-text permission (sources SRC-102–SRC-103, SRC-140, SRC-172–SRC-175).
