# Text, translation, and metadata rights

The repository's `LICENSE` applies to its software code. Project-curated metadata in the files listed by `DATA-LICENSE.md` is separately licensed under CC BY 4.0, only to the extent contributors hold rights they can license. Neither license grants blanket permission to reuse scripture transcriptions, translations, manuscript images, or other third-party content. Each text keeps the rights and attribution stated by its own source record.

**Beta maṣāḥǝft license context:** The Works TEI snapshots state file-level CC BY-SA 4.0. Current Beta maṣāḥǝft Guidelines describe project data and remixes as CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; the University of Hamburg's 2022 version 5.2 repository record carries dataset-level CC BY 4.0 metadata. The archive records all three notices, marks affected readers as `mixed`, and does not decide which notice controls or claim rights in unidentified printed exemplars. These terms remain unresolved; do not infer commercial or unrestricted permission. See `research/betamasaheft-license-audit.md`, the unsent `research/betamasaheft-rights-clarification-request.md`, and source records SRC-296–SRC-297.

The eBible.org GMVETW / `gmve` edition page identifies the Gamo Bible (`gmv`) as Ethiopic script, copyright © 2024 The Word for the World International, with The Word for the World Northern Africa listed as contributor. Its page states CC BY-SA 4.0 for the translation and requires attribution, indication of changes, and share-alike when adaptations are redistributed; images are covered by separate terms. The reader files identify the conversion and are distributed under CC BY-SA 4.0; their license does not apply to unrelated site content. Images, fonts, and CSS were excluded. The extracted chapters, markers, headings, and cross-reference notes have been checked against the captured source HTML, but wording has not been independently proofread and no canon mapping is claimed. ## Unverified multilingual repository leads

The `wuletawwonte/ethiopian-bibles` README lists Hadiyya, Nuer, and Wolaytta file labels and claims public-domain status, but its root MIT license covers software and does not grant text rights. Separate provider records now identify a 1992 Hadiyya Bible Society of Ethiopia edition, a Nuer edition marked RESERVED, and a 2004 Wolaytta SIM edition. Hadiyya reuse terms remain unknown. The Wolaytta source states CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, repeats an earlier 3.0 notice, and allows format porting only without changes to Bible words or punctuation. No scripture text is bundled. See SRC-102–SRC-103 and SRC-140, SRC-172–SRC-175.

Digital Bible Society's Tigrinya profile lists a 1998 Genesis portion attributed to the Bible Society of Eritrea. Its separate Tigrinya Common Language Bible PDF identifies Bible Society of Ethiopia copyright and says the translation metadata describes a full Bible completed in 1991, but the 133-page PDF's visible contents list the 27 New Testament titles only. The archive records both as separate unbundled discovery leads; it does not assume they are the same edition or call the PDF a verified full-Bible file. The PDF's Tigrinya notice describes noncommercial distribution by Digital Bible Society; no permission for this archive to reproduce the text was identified. See SRC-331–SRC-332. Separate Bible Society and distributor product records identify a 1998 Revised Bible in Tigrigna print edition from the Bible Society of Eritrea and describe it as a whole Bible. The printed contents and canon scope were not inspected, and the product pages grant no digital reproduction rights. This remains an unmapped, metadata-only lead with unknown reuse rights (SRC-333–SRC-334).

## Required for every new text or translation

Record the exact source/edition, original and target language tags, translator/transcriber, translation basis (including relay languages), chapter/verse boundaries, completeness checks, source citations, license text/URL, required attribution, and any restrictions. If rights are unclear or conflicting, do not bundle the text until resolved. “Free to read” and “openly licensed for reuse” are different permissions.

Language identifiers use BCP 47 tags where available. Script and text direction are metadata; they must not be inferred from a modern language label alone. Community-specific names, transliteration, and orthography should be retained alongside normalized identifiers.

## 1 Enoch source boundary and review limits

The pinned Beta maṣāḥǝft TEI record distinguishes edition 1 (Dillmann/Library of Ethiopic Texts, explicitly restricted to noncommercial use and not for sale) from the EOTCed edition, labeled “Text of the EOTC printed Bible.” The reader contains only EOTCed; edition 1 is excluded. The full 713,504-byte upstream TEI snapshot is retained locally outside the Pages site at `../source-captures/betamasaheft-enoch-2026/LIT1340EnochE-original.xml`. The site repository contains a rights-scoped EOTCed-only extract and preserves its SHA-256 in the reader and layer metadata. Structural checks confirm 108 ordered chapters and 1,058 nonempty labeled lines; chapter 21 starts at source marker 2. Source Text independently reports 1,058 verses, a count corroboration only. No printed-page/manuscript collation or line-by-line comparison was performed, and the exact printed exemplar/date is unknown. The file and Source Text record state CC BY-SA 4.0 for this selected edition scope; attribute Beta maṣāḥǝft, indicate the EOTCed-only extraction and changes, and share adaptations alike. See `readers/source/enoch/SOURCE-BOUNDARY.md` and sources SRC-040, SRC-065, and SRC-066.
